## 1. Brand Character & Design Principles

**RECOMMENDATION:** VILL Studio visual identity must embody the positioning promise: *“One vision. One scope. One accountable team”* and its core operating pillars (Design Intelligence, Execution Control, Transparency, Detail, Accountability). The brand sells risk reduction, architectural precision, and delivery certainty before decorative luxury.

### Core Visual Principles
1. **Architectural Restraint over Decorative Opulence:**  
   Avoid gold trims, ornate borders, glossy faux-3D badges, or superficial luxury clichés. Spaces and graphics are defined by structure, clean sightlines, natural textures, and geometric discipline.
2. **Material Authenticity & Tactility:**  
   Visuals must celebrate genuine textures—honed limestone, quarter-sawn oak, fluted glass, raw linen, matte powder-coated metals. Never disguise or over-saturate natural materials.
3. **Information Clarity & Structural Balance:**  
   Layouts function like architectural plans: disciplined alignment, generous functional negative space, clear typographic hierarchy, and prominent annotation of intent.
4. **Process Truth over Facade:**  
   Balance pristine completed spaces with calibrated behind-the-scenes evidence: millimetre-accurate shop drawings, material joints, site leveling, and handover quality checks.

## 2. Colour System & Material Palette

**RECOMMENDATION:** The palette draws inspiration from contemporary Egyptian architectural landscapes, natural stone quarries, and Scandinavian/Mediterranean joinery.

### Core Palette Specifications
| Role | Swatch Name | Hex Code | Material Equivalence | Application & Usage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary Base | Chalk Limestone | `#F5F3EF` | Honed Egyptian limestone / off-white plaster | Dominant canvas background (60–70% of layouts), clean negative space |
| Primary Dark | Basalt Slate | `#1E2022` | Deep charcoal / dark honed granite | Primary text, high-contrast framing, master logo mark |
| Architectural Neutral | Raw Mortar | `#DDD8D0` | Warm light-grey travertine / linen | Secondary cards, borders, dividers, subtle architectural gridlines |
| Material Accent | Smoked Walnut | `#5C4A3E` | Deep oiled European walnut joinery | Secondary headers, structural accents, warmth indicators |
| Earth Accent | Muted Terracotta | `#9E5D4E` | Baked clay / natural kiln brick | Restrained focal pop (≤5%): callouts, pins, measurement tags |

### Contrast & Accessibility Rules
- Dark text (`#1E2022`) on light base (`#F5F3EF`) yields a 13.5:1 contrast ratio (exceeding WCAG AAA).
- Terracotta accent (`#9E5D4E`) is strictly used on `#F5F3EF` backgrounds or as a subtle structural tint; never use low-contrast text overlays.

## 3. Typographic System (Arabic & Latin Pairing)

**RECOMMENDATION:** Disciplined, contemporary dual-script system engineered for architectural clarity across digital interfaces, social feeds, and technical presentations.

### Font Pairing
- **Primary Latin Typeface:** *Neue Haas Grotesk* (alternative: *Inter* or *Suisse Int'l*). Clean, neo-grotesque structural rhythm with precise numerals for technical measurements.
- **Primary Arabic Typeface:** *Noto Sans Arabic* or *IBM Plex Sans Arabic*. Modern, legible sans-serif structure that harmonizes in stroke weight and x-height with contemporary Latin grotesques without calligraphic ornamentation.

### Hierarchy & Scale Guidelines
| Level | Latin Weight / Size | Arabic Weight / Size | Letter Spacing & Line Height | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hero / Display | Medium · 48–64pt | Bold / Medium · 42–56pt | -0.02em tracking; 1.15 line-height | Campaign hero claims, large poster statements |
| Primary Header (H1/H2) | Medium · 28–36pt | Medium · 26–32pt | -0.01em tracking; 1.25 line-height | Service names, case study titles, landing headers |
| Subhead / Technical Tag | Regular / Medium · 16–20pt | Regular / Medium · 15–18pt | 0.00em tracking; 1.35 line-height | Pillar markers, stage identifiers, architectural callouts |
| Body Copy | Regular · 14–16pt | Regular · 14–16pt | Normal tracking; 1.50 line-height | Explanatory copy, process notes, BOQ details |
| Micro / Data Label | Medium · 11–12pt (Monospace optional) | Medium · 11–12pt | +0.04em tracking; 1.30 line-height | Dimensions (mm/m²), timeline dates, QC check labels |

## 4. Photography, Render & Image Direction

**RECOMMENDATION:** Photography and 3D visualizations must feel grounded in real physical space, avoiding hyperbolic lighting and synthetic filters.

### Photographic Rules
- **Camera Perspective:** Strict one-point or two-point perspective aligned to architectural axes. Eye-level or waist-level camera height (100–120 cm from finished floor). Avoid extreme wide-angle distortion (keep focal length between 24mm and 50mm full-frame equivalent).
- **Lighting & Atmosphere:** Soft, diffused natural daylight as the key source. Warm interior accent lighting (2700K–3000K) turned on only to indicate function. No dramatic colored party lights or high-contrast HDR halos.
- **Detail & Macro Focus:** Capture the joint, the edge, the joinery transition, the grain matching, the shadow gap, and the flush handle detail.
- **Render Labeling Standard:** Per BRIEF and Strategy Baseline §2: Every 3D generation or rendering must feature a clear, discreet label: `[3D Concept Visualization]` or `[التصميم الافتراضي]`. Only handover-documented physical photography may be titled `Completed Project / مشروع منفذ`.

## 5. Layout, Grid & Social Asset Framework

**RECOMMENDATION:** Strict grid discipline across social (1:1, 4:5, 9:16) and web touchpoints, reflecting architectural drawings and technical portfolios.

### Grid & Composition Rules
- **Margins & Safe Zones:** Generous 8–10% outer safe zone on all edges.
- **The Split-Screen Rule:** Effective for Before/After, Plan/Execution, or Render/Built comparisons: clean 50/50 vertical or horizontal divide with a 1px `#DDD8D0` hairline separator and architectural dimension tags.
- **Technical Overlay Language:** Incorporate subtle architectural annotations: hairline dimension lines (`← 2400 mm →`), material labels (`Matte Lacquer · Oak Veneer`), and inspection check-tags (`QC Verified · Milestone 03`).

### Asset Format Specifications
1. **Carousel (4:5 / 1080×1350 px):**  
   Slide 1: Clean hero architectural image + title overlay.  
   Slides 2–5: Deep-dive sequence (Problem → Technical Decision → Material Selection → Site Execution).  
   Final Slide: Summary scope tag + CTA (`Project Assessment`).
2. **Reels / Short-form Video (9:16 / 1080×1920 px):**  
   Clean lower-third title cards in Chalk Limestone on Basalt Slate. Zero bouncy sticker animations. Deliberate, smooth camera movements (slow dolly or pan); cuts timed to natural narrative beats.
3. **Stills / Single Ads (1:1 & 4:5):**  
   One strong focal point. Negative space occupies at least 30% of the frame. Header positioned at top-left (LTR) or top-right (RTL); discreet logo anchor at corner.

## 6. Creative Containment: Kitchen Paid Entry vs Whole-Home Studio

**RECOMMENDATION:** In direct response to the Strategy Baseline v0.2 §3 containment mandate:

- **The Visual Rule:** Paid kitchen ad creative must visually look like a page from the VILL Studio master architecture monograph, *never* like an independent discount kitchen showroom or generic appliance catalogue.
- **Identity Anchoring:**  
  Every kitchen campaign asset carries the primary VILL Studio master mark:
  `VILL STUDIO · Interior Design & Fit-out`  
  accompanied by a secondary project descriptor:  
  `[The Kitchen Space · Integrated Architectural Scope]`.
- **Stylistic Continuity:**  
  Kitchen visuals must demonstrate how the kitchen connects to the adjoining living/dining areas (sightlines, flooring transitions, coordinated joinery finishes). This preserves whole-home credibility while serving the productized kitchen acquisition wedge.

## 8. Brand Dos and Don'ts

| Category | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| **Tone & Claims** | Explain the technical decision behind the aesthetic; cite verifiable processes; use restrained, architectural language. | Do not use superlative luxury jargon ("Exclusive luxury", "Unmatched royalty", "Cheapest prices"). |
| **Visual Elements** | Use hairline structural dividers, technical dimension annotations, and generous negative space. | Do not use decorative flourish badges, drop shadows, gradient borders, or 3D gold buttons. |
| **Material Depiction** | Showcase authentic, matte, tactile materials with realistic joints and grain textures. | Do not show hyper-glossy, synthetic, plastic-looking finishes or oversaturated lens flares. |
| **Campaign Integration** | Frame kitchen projects as an integral architectural zone connected to the wider home. | Do not isolate the kitchen like a standalone appliance shop or retail cabinet seller. |
| **Image Integrity** | Explicitly label all 3D visualizations as concepts; use authentic site logs for execution proof. | Do not present 3D renders as finished handover projects; never use generic stock photography. |
