# TSST-CREATIVE-SK-008 Candidate Rationale and Visual QA

Fixture: `NABD-ART-DIRECTION-v1.0`  
Scope: fictional internal lab; not published and not production-validated.

## Brief trace

- Audience: municipal operations directors, network engineers, and infrastructure-program executives.
- Decision: request a technical assessment or demo.
- Promise: “Find pressure loss before it becomes water loss.” / “اكتشف هبوط الضغط قبل أن يتحول إلى فاقد مياه.”
- Proof: one operational view joins pressure signals, inspection priority, ownership, and the decision trail.
- Character: observant, civic-minded, exact, and calm under pressure.
- Constraints applied: no customer/site implication, no savings claims, no fear imagery, no droplets, no generic SaaS gradient, no neon, no logo-finality claim, and no literal dashboard screenshot.
- Surfaces: social feed, 16:9 executive slide, desktop hero, mobile hero. English and Arabic receive independent, credible reading directions and comparable prominence.

## Three genuinely different directions

### 01 — Civic Cutaway

Strategic idea: reveal the system beneath the civic surface. The distinctive device is a sectional material field crossed by one buried signal path and one circular inspection aperture. Editorial asymmetry, mineral neutrals, a single terracotta alert color, dense bilingual headlines, and generous quiet zones create a calm observational tone. Its risk is becoming overly cartographic if labels multiply.

### 02 — Operational Ledger

Strategic idea: turn an invisible network into a disciplined chain of operational evidence. Its device is a ruled, physical field record with columns, check marks, stamps, and progressive density—not a software dashboard. Monochrome-first typography creates credibility. Its risk is reduced emotional immediacy and weaker feed-scale distinctiveness.

### 03 — Pressure Echo

Strategic idea: reveal loss through changes in the spacing and tension of contour echoes. The device is an off-axis radial field with one interrupted warm pulse. Large typography remains outside the field; deep civic green supports a calm, atmospheric feel. Its risk is that the infrastructure meaning is less immediate without context.

## Selection

**Selected: 01 — Civic Cutaway.** It most directly answers “make hidden infrastructure legible” without imitating a dashboard. The surface/system/signal relationship is visible before any label is read, and the path-plus-aperture grammar adapts cleanly to a vertical social composition, a decision-first slide, and responsive hero crops. The Ledger was rejected because it privileges process over hidden infrastructure and becomes documentation-heavy at social scale. Pressure Echo was rejected because it is ownable but more abstract and less immediately municipal.

## Selected-system rules

- Typography: English uses compact, heavy, left-aligned headlines; Arabic uses a separate RTL block with comparable visual weight and natural line breaks. Never mirror punctuation or force both languages into one line.
- Composition: copy occupies a quiet civic surface; the cutaway occupies a separate field. One oblique buried path creates the scan line and one aperture creates the focal point.
- Imagery: abstract material strata only; no identifiable customer site. Warm daylight/mineral behavior, not disaster lighting.
- Color: sand `#F3EAD8`, deep civic green `#173B3D`, mineral green `#8FA99E`, and terracotta signal `#D86B43`. The hierarchy still works in monochrome through scale, placement, geometry, and contrast.
- Spacing: outer safe areas are at least 5% on social/slide; desktop uses 64 px gutters; mobile uses 20 px gutters. Labels stay out of the pipe/aperture focal area.
- Consistency: the cutaway strata, oblique pressure path, circular aperture, quiet copy zone, and one warm signal repeat across every channel. Density and order change by surface rather than mechanically resizing.

## Channel adaptation

- Social: vertical crop, headline-first scan, then Arabic, then the cutaway focal point and CTA. The image is intentionally compressed into the lower field for feed-scale impact.
- Executive slide: decision-first bilingual promise and approved proof stay on the projector-safe left; the evidence chain anchors the bottom; the cutaway occupies the right third.
- Desktop hero: two-column composition with CTA hierarchy and neutral operational labels. The visual field runs full height to make the buried-path mechanism immersive.
- Mobile hero: reading order becomes lockup → English promise → Arabic promise → two CTA treatments → cropped cutaway. The aperture remains visible while the strata and path crop intentionally.

- Focal hierarchy: checked on all renders. Primary promise is first, Arabic is second but comparably prominent, the aperture is the visual focal point, and CTA/action is clear.
- Originality: checked. The work avoids stock imagery, ornamental grids, playful droplets, generic gradients, and literal product UI.
- Cross-channel coherence: checked. All four applications retain the path/aperture/strata grammar while changing crop, density, and sequence.
- Bilingual readability: checked in rendered PNGs for intact Arabic shaping, RTL alignment, adequate size, and non-overlapping line breaks.
- Image quality: vector/CSS-generated material fields remain sharp at native render sizes; no generated text or fictional site imagery is embedded.
- Alignment and edge safety: checked. Social and slide maintain generous margins; desktop uses 64 px gutters; mobile uses 20 px gutters and keeps all content within the 390 px viewport.
- Production behavior: desktop and mobile are separate responsive states rather than a scaled duplicate. Slide density is projector-safe and proof copy is legible.
- Claims: checked against the fixture. Only approved copy and neutral operational labels are used on channel applications.

## Unresolved production risks

- These are lab renders, not accessibility or production validation. Final font licensing, browser/device testing, minimum contrast, keyboard/focus states, and localization review remain required.
- The lab lockup is typographic and must not be treated as a final logo.
- The abstract cutaway is intentionally fictional and should not be presented as a real municipal network or customer location.
