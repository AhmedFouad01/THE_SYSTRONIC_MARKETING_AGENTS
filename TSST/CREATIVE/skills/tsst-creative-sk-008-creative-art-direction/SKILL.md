# TSST-CREATIVE-SK-008 Creative Art Direction

Status: `LAB_VALIDATED` — `TSST-CREATIVE-EV-011`, 88/100

## Objective

Turn an approved brand brief into a distinctive visual idea that governs imagery, typography, composition, color behavior, space, and channel adaptation. Prove the direction through finished-looking applications rather than mood words alone.

## Required method

1. Extract the audience, decision, promise, proof, personality, constraints, languages, and channel needs from the supplied brief.
2. Develop two or three directions that differ in concept and visual mechanism—not just palette, prompt wording, or photography subject.
3. Define each direction through a name, strategic idea, key visual device, composition, typography behavior, imagery/lighting, palette behavior, spacing/rhythm, and risks.
4. Select one direction against the brief. State why it is more ownable and useful than the alternatives and what was rejected.
5. Execute the selected system on the required channels at realistic proportions. Preserve the concept while adapting hierarchy, density, crop, and reading order.
6. Inspect the rendered outputs, correct obvious defects, and record visual QA findings.

## Output contract

- brief trace and constraints;
- two or three visibly distinct direction boards;
- selected direction with comparative rationale;
- rendered social, executive-slide, and responsive-web-hero applications;
- Arabic/English treatment;
- explicit typography hierarchy, composition, imagery, spacing, and consistency rules;
- visual QA evidence and unresolved production risks.

## Art-direction standard

A direction must contain an organizing idea that is recognizable without its color palette. Typography, image crop, geometry, material/texture, and negative space should reinforce that idea. Avoid generic centered headline plus gradient, interchangeable stock imagery, ornamental technology grids, and channel layouts that are merely resized.

## Cross-channel requirements

- Social: immediate focal point and message hierarchy at feed scale.
- Executive slide: decision-first title, credible information structure, and projector-safe density.
- Responsive web hero: clear value proposition, CTA hierarchy, resilient reflow, and intentional mobile crop/order.
- Arabic and English: meaning parity, correct RTL/LTR behavior, credible line breaks, and comparable prominence.

## Visual QA

Review the actual renders for:

- focal hierarchy and scan path;
- concept originality and non-generic character;
- brand coherence and repeated visual grammar;
- cross-channel consistency without mechanical resizing;
- Arabic/English readability and typographic hierarchy;
- image quality, crop, lighting, and subject relevance;
- spacing, alignment, rhythm, and edge safety;
- realistic production behavior, including mobile reflow and slide legibility;
- accidental text/image artifacts, unsupported claims, or imitation of a reference brand.

## References to style sheet (L3)

1. Open every reference; list what was NOT opened.
2. Take: set, light direction and hardness, camera height/angle, framing, negative space, one-subject rule, collection-by-colour.
3. Never copy: layouts, products, typography, their logo.
4. Output a style section including a CAN/CANNOT gap block: the user's real assets vs the benchmark.

## Guardrails

- Art direction is more than an image-generation prompt or a moodboard.
- Do not copy a reference brand's distinctive identity.
- Do not invent product proof, customer results, or legal/accessibility clearance.
- Do not hide weak hierarchy behind decorative effects.
- Do not claim production validation from a lab render.

## Practical evaluation

Given one fictional bilingual brand brief, create three distinct directions, select one with rationale, and render it as a social ad, executive slide, and responsive web hero. Score the actual visuals for hierarchy, originality, coherence, cross-channel consistency, readability, production realism, and whether the work is generic or genuinely art-directed.
