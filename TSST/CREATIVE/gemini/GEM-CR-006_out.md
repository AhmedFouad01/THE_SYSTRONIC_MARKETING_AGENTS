# Typography Craft Reference for Social & Brand Design

## PART 1 — Hierarchy craft for social images

**Scale and Proportion**
*   **Scale steps:** Use a mathematical typographic scale to determine sizes. The Major Third (1.25 ratio) or Perfect Fourth (1.333 ratio) are standard for clean hierarchy. For dramatic impact in marketing, use the Golden Ratio (1.618). [VISME p.91] notes that sizing objects according to their importance dictates visual hierarchy.
*   **Size contrast ratios:** Multiply your base size (e.g., body text at 30pt) by the scale ratio. E.g., if body is 30pt, an H2 might be 40pt (1.333x), and an H1 might be 65pt (1.618x). 
*   **Minimum Size:** [VISME p.23] Use a point size no smaller than 30 for presentations and social posts to ensure legibility on mobile screens. Short titles should be uppercase.
*   **Text Amount:** [VISME p.22] Limit text to the absolute minimum necessary so it reads quickly. [VISME p.68] advises spreading content out across multiple slides/carousel frames instead of cramming text into one image.

**Spacing (Leading and Tracking)**
*   **Leading (Line Height):** [VISME p.24] Adjust leading so text can breathe; default software leading is often too high or tight. For display/H1 text, use tighter leading (e.g., 100%–110% of font size). For body text or small labels, use looser leading (130%–150%). 
*   **Tracking (Letter Spacing):** Tracking should tighten as font size grows. Large headlines need negative tracking (e.g., -20 to -40 em/1000) to look cohesive. Small body text needs neutral tracking.
*   **All-Caps Labels:** Small, all-caps text (like "NEW ARRIVAL" or kickers) must have wide, positive tracking (e.g., +100 to +200 em/1000) for readability and a premium feel.

**Alignment and Formatting**
*   **Optical alignment:** Align text blocks optically, not mathematically. Use **hanging punctuation** (pushing quotes or bullets outside the text box) so the letters align perfectly on the left margin. Account for **overshoot** (curved letters like O or C extending slightly past the baseline/cap-height to look optically equal in size to flat letters like H).
*   **Alignment choice:** [VISME p.96] Left-aligned text is the standard for readability and directing the eye. Center alignment can be used for short titles to provide balance, but never center-align paragraphs longer than 3 lines.
*   **Line breaks:** Break lines manually by phrasing and meaning, not just where the text box ends. 
*   **Widows and Orphans:** In 2-3 line headlines, never leave a single word alone on the last line (a widow). Adjust the line break or tracking to bring another word down.
*   **Line length:** The optimal line length for readable body text is 45–75 characters per line.


## PART 2 — Mixed-type lockups (most important)

### a) Script/handwritten + bold sans (or serif) lockups
This lockup places a flowing script accent word interacting with a structured, bold base word (e.g., a handwritten "Premium" overlapping a bold "FURNITURE").

| Variable | Recipe |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Size Ratio** | The script word should be ~30–50% the height of the bold word. |
| **Overlap %** | Overlap by 10% to 25% of the bold word's height to create interaction without destroying legibility. |
| **Layer Order** | Script on top is standard. Bold on top (with script peeking out from behind) requires a thicker script and careful positioning so the script remains readable. |
| **Colour Contrast** | High contrast. E.g., Bold word in dark petrol (`#011C2C`), script word in brand orange (`#F58021`). |
| **Rotation/Offset** | Rotate the script 5° to 12° upward for a natural, handwritten feel. |
| **Word Limit** | Max 1-2 script words interacting with 1-3 bold words. |

*   **When to use:** For campaign titles, short promotional hooks, or adding a human, artisanal touch to a corporate brand.
*   **Mistakes:** Using a script font that is too thin (gets lost), overlapping too much so the bold word becomes unreadable, or rotating the script downwards (looks depressing).
*   **Premium vs Cheap:** Premium lockups respect the baseline and use optical kerning. Cheap lockups use unedited script fonts with messy ligatures and poor contrast.

### b) Other lockups
*   **Serif italic accent inside a sans headline:** (e.g., "The *art* of living"). The italic word should be sized 110% of the sans words to match optical x-height.
*   **Weight contrast within one family:** [VISME p.26] Pair a Black weight with a Thin weight from the same typeface to create instant, harmonious contrast.
*   **Outline + solid:** The top half of a word in solid color, the bottom half (or a repeating echo) in a 1px to 2px stroke outline.

### c) Examples
1.  **Script + Bold Lockup:** [Typism Community Showcase](https://www.typismcommunity.com) — Shows how script weight must hold its own against the bold base.
2.  **Weight Contrast:** [Fonts In Use: Inter](https://fontsinuse.com/typefaces/108920/inter) — Notice how bold and light weights of Inter separate information hierarchically.
3.  **Italic Accent:** [Behance: Typography Lockup](https://www.behance.net/search/projects/?search=Typography+lockup) — Look for editorial layouts combining a serif italic accent inside a geometric sans-serif block.

### d) Arabic equivalents
*   **The Mix:** Pair a fluid, handwritten, or classical calligraphy style (like Ruqaa or Diwani) as an accent overlapping a geometric or modern Kufi bold base.
*   **What breaks:** Be extremely careful not to overlap the "dots" (nuqat) or the connecting strokes (kashida) of the Arabic script, as this destroys legibility. The accent word must overlap on the empty vertical spaces of the Kufi letters (like Alif or Lam).
*   **Examples:** [Khatt Foundation - Arabic Typography](https://www.khtt.net/en/page/1138/arabic-typography) — Showcases modern Kufi paired with fluid scripts.


## PART 3 — Pairing

**Rules for Pairing**
*   **Contrast vs Harmony:** [VISME p.27] Stick to a maximum of 2-3 typefaces per design. [VISME p.31] Create contrast by mixing a serif with a sans-serif, or varying sizes and weights. [VISME p.30] Never pair two fonts that look similar (e.g., two geometric sans-serifs), as it looks like a mistake rather than intentional contrast.
*   **Distinct Roles:** [VISME p.28] Assign a distinct role to each font (e.g., Font A for headlines, Font B for body text). 
*   **Matching x-heights:** When mixing fonts on the same line, adjust the point size so their x-heights (the height of lowercase letters) match optically, not just mathematically.

**VILL Brand Pairing: Inter + Alexandria**
*   **Size ratio:** Alexandria often runs slightly smaller optically than Inter. You may need to set Alexandria at 105% of Inter's size when placed side-by-side to match visual size.
*   **Weights:** Alexandria SemiBold pairs well with Inter Bold to match visual color/weight on the page.
*   **Line-height:** Arabic scripts require ~15% more line height (leading) than Latin scripts to accommodate taller ascenders and deeper descenders.

**Latin Hand/Script Fonts (Google Fonts, OFL)**
To pair with Inter for a quiet premium brand:
1.  [**Style Script**](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Style+Script) (OFL): A clean, mid-century script. Best at 60pt+. Risk: Can look retro if misused.
2.  [**Alex Brush**](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Alex+Brush) (OFL): Elegant, formal brush script. Best at 72pt+. Risk: Thin strokes can get lost on busy backgrounds.
3.  [**Tangerine**](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Tangerine) (OFL): Elegant, tall ascenders. Best at 80pt+. Risk: Needs very generous line height.
4.  [**Dancing Script**](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Dancing+Script) (OFL): Lively but legible. Best at 48pt+. Risk: Slightly informal for a very strict corporate brand.
5.  [**Pacifico**](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Pacifico) (OFL): Thick brush script. Best at 50pt+. Risk: Leans casual/fun rather than premium.
6.  [**Caveat**](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Caveat) (OFL): Clean handwriting. Best at 40pt+. Risk: Looks like a personal note, not a logo.
7.  [**Great Vibes**](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Great+Vibes) (OFL): Highly formal script. Best at 72pt+. Risk: Very complex loops.
8.  [**Satisfy**](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Satisfy) (OFL): A brush script with a bit of flair. Best at 50pt+. Risk: Can feel heavy.

**Arabic Hand/Script Fonts (Google Fonts, OFL)**
To pair with Alexandria:
1.  [**Aref Ruqaa**](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Aref+Ruqaa) (OFL): Classical Ruqaa style, informal handwriting feel. Best at 50pt+. Risk: Very traditional.
2.  [**Rakkas**](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Rakkas) (OFL): Bold, expressive, display handwriting. Best at 60pt+. Risk: Very heavy.
3.  [**Lemonada**](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Lemonada) (OFL): Modern, slightly casual display. Best at 40pt+. Risk: Wide proportions.
4.  [**Lalezar**](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Lalezar) (OFL): Thick, display Arabic. Best at 60pt+. Risk: Designed for impact, not subtle elegance.


## PART 4 — Type on photos

**Placement**
*   [VISME p.46-47] Place text on empty, uncluttered planes (sky, walls, floors) or intentionally blurred sections to make text pop.
*   [VISME p.48] Use elements in the photo (like the direction a subject is looking or architectural leading lines) to guide the viewer's eye to the text.
*   [VISME p.100] Use negative space to emphasize the text; let the text breathe away from the subject.

**Contrast Without Overlays**
*   *Note: [VISME p.54] recommends using shape overlays or transparent scrims to aid readability. However, TSST brand rules forbid color overlays/scrims on project photos to preserve realism and premium feel.*
*   **Sampling:** Sample a dark/light tone from the photo itself, then adjust it to be significantly darker or lighter to hit a 4.5:1 contrast ratio for the text. Do not use pure brand orange `#F58021` if it clashes violently with the photo's natural palette.
*   **Shadows:** If text sits on a slightly varied background, use a very subtle drop shadow (Opacity 15-20%, Blur 20px, Y-offset 5px, Distance 0, Color: dark petrol `#011C2C`). *Mistake:* Harsh, black, 100% opacity shadows look instantly cheap.
*   **Weight:** Use Black or ExtraBold weights if the background has mild texture.

**Examples of Clean Type on Photos**
*   [Fonts In Use - Editorial Design](https://fontsinuse.com/tags/30/editorial-design) — Notice how text finds the "quiet" areas of photography without relying on dark boxes.


## PART 5 — Arabic + English together (RTL/LTR)

**Bilingual Lockups**
*   **Stacked (One above the other):** Most reliable. Place the primary language on top. Align both to the same side based on the primary language's direction, OR center-align them if they are short titles.
*   **Side-by-side:** Separate them with a visual delimiter (a pipe `|` or a 1px vertical line). Provide generous padding.
*   **Mixing:** When inserting a Latin brand name (e.g., "VILL") inside an Arabic sentence, ensure the bidi (bidirectional) text engine handles it correctly so the Arabic sentence continues properly from right to left.
*   **Associations:** [VISME p.102] Use the same color or visual weight for the translated pair so viewers instantly associate them as the same information.

**Alignment and Numerals**
*   **Baseline:** Arabic scripts visually sit differently on the baseline and have deeper descenders. Do not force mathematical alignment. Visually nudge the Arabic layer up or down by a few pixels until the weights look balanced.
*   **Numerals:** Choose either Western (1, 2, 3) or Arabic-Indic (١, ٢, ٣) and stick to it strictly within the same sentence. Do not mix numeral types.
*   **Punctuation:** Be careful at boundaries; parentheses and periods often jump to the wrong side of the string in design software if RTL direction is not strictly enforced.


## PART 6 — Detailing checklist

A 25-item pre-export checklist for premium typography:

**Hierarchy & Spacing**
1. [ ] Is the focal point immediately obvious? [VISME p.99]
2. [ ] Are there maximum 3 typefaces used? [VISME p.27]
3. [ ] Does the text meet a minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratio?
4. [ ] Is the leading tight on headlines (100-110%)? [VISME p.24]
5. [ ] Is the leading open on body text (130-150%)?
6. [ ] Is the tracking tightened on large display text?
7. [ ] Is the tracking wide/positive on all-caps labels?
8. [ ] Are line lengths between 45-75 characters?
9. [ ] Are line breaks manual and based on phrasing?
10. [ ] Are there zero widows or orphans?

**Alignment & Detailing**
11. [ ] Is the text optically aligned on the left margin?
12. [ ] Is hanging punctuation applied?
13. [ ] Are curved letters overshooting the baseline slightly?
14. [ ] Are smart quotes used (`“ ”` and `’`) instead of straight/dumb quotes (`" "` and `'`)?
15. [ ] Are em-dashes (`—`) used for breaks, and en-dashes (`–`) for ranges?
16. [ ] Are true ellipses (`…`) used instead of three periods (`...`)?
17. [ ] Are ligatures enabled for the serif/script fonts?
18. [ ] Are tabular numerals used for data/pricing, and proportional for body?

**Mixed & Bilingual Type**
19. [ ] In lockups, is the script word rotated slightly (5-12°)?
20. [ ] Do the script and bold words overlap without destroying legibility?
21. [ ] Are Arabic and Latin font weights matched visually, not just by font-menu weight?
22. [ ] Is the Arabic line height increased by ~15% to prevent clipping?
23. [ ] Are numerals consistent (Western vs Arabic-Indic)?
24. [ ] Does the text sit in a natural "quiet plane" on the photo?
25. [ ] *Project specific:* Are there absolutely NO color overlays or scrims on the photo?


## PART 7 — 10 training exercises

1. **Exercise 1: Optical Alignment**
   * *Input:* A left-aligned headline starting with a quote: `"Premium Design"`.
   * *Target:* Hang the quotation mark entirely outside the left bounding box.
   * *Pass Check:* A straight vertical line drawn down the left margin perfectly touches the 'P' and the 'D'.
2. **Exercise 2: Scale and Ratio**
   * *Input:* Body text at 24pt.
   * *Target:* Create an H2 and H1 using the Perfect Fourth (1.333) scale.
   * *Pass Check:* H2 is ~32pt, H1 is ~42pt or 43pt.
3. **Exercise 3: Tracking by Size**
   * *Input:* H1 at 80pt, Body at 24pt, Label (all caps) at 16pt.
   * *Target:* Adjust tracking for all three.
   * *Pass Check:* H1 has negative tracking (e.g., -20), Body is 0, Label is positive (e.g., +150).
4. **Exercise 4: The Script Lockup**
   * *Input:* Words: "The" (Script), "FUTURE" (Bold Sans).
   * *Target:* Create a premium mixed-type lockup.
   * *Pass Check:* "The" is 40% the size of "FUTURE", rotated 8°, overlaps "FUTURE" by 15%, and is placed in front.
5. **Exercise 5: Bilingual Matching**
   * *Input:* English "VILL Design" (Inter, 40pt) and Arabic "تصميم فيل" (Alexandria, 40pt).
   * *Target:* Match them visually side-by-side.
   * *Pass Check:* Alexandria size increased to ~42pt, baseline nudged so the optical centers align.
6. **Exercise 6: Finding the Quiet Plane**
   * *Input:* A complex photo of a living room with a plain ceiling.
   * *Target:* Place the text without using an overlay or box.
   * *Pass Check:* Text is located entirely within the plain ceiling area, and color is sampled from a dark shadow in the room for contrast.
7. **Exercise 7: Avoiding Widows**
   * *Input:* A text box where the word "design." falls alone on the third line.
   * *Target:* Fix the paragraph layout.
   * *Pass Check:* Manual line break inserted earlier so the last line reads "premium design."
8. **Exercise 8: Proper Punctuation**
   * *Input:* Text: `The 1980's -- a "golden" era...`
   * *Target:* Convert to professional typographic punctuation.
   * *Pass Check:* Text reads: `The 1980s — a “golden” era…` (apostrophe removed for plural decades, em-dash used, smart quotes used, true ellipsis used).
9. **Exercise 9: Contrast Testing**
   * *Input:* Orange text (`#F58021`) on a light gray wall background.
   * *Target:* Adjust colors to pass WCAG AA.
   * *Pass Check:* Text changed to dark petrol (`#011C2C`) to achieve a contrast ratio >= 4.5:1.
10. **Exercise 10: Hierarchy via Weight (No Size Change)**
    * *Input:* "Project 01: Modern Villa" all in Inter Regular 30pt.
    * *Target:* Establish hierarchy using only font weight and color.
    * *Pass Check:* "Project 01:" is Inter Regular in brand orange (`#F58021`), "Modern Villa" is Inter Black in dark petrol (`#011C2C`).
