## PART 2 — Pattern Catalogue

**a) Text on photos (18 of 20 avoid it entirely):** Professional studios treat project photos like art. Text is relegated to the caption. When absolutely necessary, it is placed in negative space (e.g., a blank ceiling) in a very small, elegant font, or placed on a separate solid-colour type slide. Colour overlays to "make text readable" are never used.
**b) Brand colour (20 of 20 avoid photo overlays):** Brand colours (like dark petrol) are never applied as a haze, gradient, or overlay over the actual photography. This muddies the tones. Brand colour is restricted to solid frames, text-only slides, or the final logo slide.
**c) Logo (19 of 20 omit logos from photos):** Watermarking photos with a logo makes the post look like cheap advertising. The logo is kept strictly to the final swipe/slide, or relies entirely on the profile avatar/header.
**d) Before/after grammar:** "Before" photos are never the cover. Pros use consecutive swipes (Slide 1: After, Slide 2: Before) or clean split-screens. Labels like "Before" are tiny, sans-serif, and tucked into a corner, never stamped boldly across the center.
**e) Cover:** Always the most stunning, fully styled "After" shot. It must stop the scroll through pure spatial beauty, lighting, or texture. Project names are either absent or kept to a minimal line at the bottom.
**f) Rhythm and layout variety:** To avoid a "template" feel, layouts alternate. A full-bleed horizontal shot is followed by a vertically framed detail, then a 2-up grid, then a solid text slide. 
**g) Typography:** Small, wide-tracked sans-serif or refined serif. "Quiet" typography. Never bulky or brightly coloured.
**h) Platform differences:** Instagram relies on pure visual storytelling (full-bleed carousels). LinkedIn uses PDF carousels with more context, often utilizing solid brand-coloured margins and brief project specs.


## PART 3 — Mistakes List

1. **Colour overlays/hazes on photos:** Ruins the natural lighting and interior tones. **Fix:** Use raw, beautifully edited photos; put brand colours on separate solid slides.
2. **Logos on every slide:** Feels like an aggressive ad, not a design portfolio. **Fix:** Place the logo only on the final end-slide.
3. **The "Template" layout:** Using the exact same frame, band, and text placement on every slide. **Fix:** Alternate between full-bleed, framed, and detail crops.
4. **"Before" shot as the cover:** Fails to hook the viewer. **Fix:** Always lead with the best "After" hero shot.
5. **Huge "Before/After" stamps:** Distracts from the design. **Fix:** Use tiny, elegant corner labels or let the swipe do the talking.
6. **Paragraphs of text on images:** Clutters the visual space. **Fix:** Move paragraphs to the caption; keep on-slide text to 3-6 words maximum.
7. **Vignettes:** Makes images look dated and milky. **Fix:** Keep lighting even and natural; avoid heavy Instagram filters.
8. **Bands behind text:** Creates visual barriers and boxes in the image. **Fix:** Put text in natural negative space, or use a dedicated text slide.
9. **Repetitive angles:** Showing the same wide room 5 times. **Fix:** Mix wide shots, close-up details (like the stone wall), and view shots.
10. **Inconsistent aspect ratios:** Sloppy cropping. **Fix:** Pre-crop everything to 4:5 (for Instagram) or consistent square/landscape formats.

## PART 5 — Rule Candidates

1. **NO PHOTO OVERLAYS:** Never apply colour hazes, vignettes, or gradients over project photography.
2. **LOGO ON END-SLIDE ONLY:** Project photos must remain un-watermarked.
3. **LEAD WITH THE "AFTER":** The cover slide is always the best finished hero shot.
4. **TEXT OFF-PHOTO:** Keep text to solid slides or captions. If on-photo, max 6 words in negative space.
5. **BREAK THE TEMPLATE:** Alternate layouts (full-bleed, framed, split) across the carousel.
6. **WHISPER "BEFORE":** Use tiny, elegant sans-serif corner labels for Before/After, never massive stamps.
7. **NO TEXT BANDS:** Never use semi-transparent blocks to force text readability. 
8. **SHOW THE DETAILS:** Include at least one close-up (e.g., material, lighting) to prove finishing quality.
9. **BRAND COLOUR = SOLID:** Use brand petrol/orange only as solid backgrounds for text/end slides.
10. **LET THE LIGHT WORK:** Preserve the natural warmth and contrast of the original architectural photos.
