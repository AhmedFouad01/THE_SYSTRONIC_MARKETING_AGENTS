# GEM-CR-004: Deep Research on Short-Form Video & AI Production

## PART 1 — Storytelling for short-form video and ads

### 1. Hook in the first 1–3 s
The first 1 to 3 seconds determine viewer retention across all platforms.
- **Meta (Facebook/Instagram):** Users decide to stop or scroll in ~1.7 seconds. Visual changes must occur within the first 100–200 milliseconds to break the "scroll reflex." Three-layer hooks (visuals, audio, text) perform best.
- **TikTok:** Over 70% of users decide to scroll within 3 seconds. Videos achieving a 60–70% 3-second retention rate often receive 4 to 7 times more algorithmic impressions. 
- **YouTube Shorts (ABCD Framework):** "Attention" requires jumping straight into the action with tight framing and fast-paced immersion.
- **Hook Types:** 
  - *Visual/Pattern Interrupt:* Fast zooms, immediate motion, or an anomaly.
  - *Question/Curiosity Gap:* "Have you been doing this wrong?"
  - *Tension:* High-energy or high-stakes opening frame.
  - *Before/After:* Immediate visual contrast.
  - *Reveal:* Hinting at a secret or final product early on.

### 2. Story structures (6 / 15 / 30 / 60 s)

#### Before & After (Transformation)
- **Lengths:** 6s, 15s.
- **Beat Sheet:** 
  - *0-2s:* The Hook (The dramatic "Before" state).
  - *2-4s:* The Catalyst (The action or product introducing change).
  - *4-6s:* The "After" / Reveal.
- **When to use:** Highly visual services (interior design, renovations).
- **Common mistake:** Spending too much time on the "Before," causing drop-off.

#### Problem & Solution
- **Lengths:** 15s, 30s.
- **Beat Sheet:** 
  - *0-3s:* Hook (Pain point stated directly).
  - *3-10s:* Agitation & Empathy (Why the problem is frustrating).
  - *10-20s:* The Solution (Introduction of the product/service).
  - *20-30s:* Proof & CTA (Results and what to do next).
- **When to use:** Paid ads targeting specific audience pain points.
- **Common mistake:** Using a "feature-first" opening instead of a "problem-first" hook.

#### Process / Behind-the-Scenes (BTS)
- **Lengths:** 30s, 60s.
- **Beat Sheet:** 
  - *0-3s:* Hook (Glimpse of the final result or an engaging action).
  - *3-15s:* The Setup (Raw materials, planning, the "messy middle").
  - *15-45s:* The Craft (Time-lapse, satisfying actions, attention to detail).
  - *45-60s:* The Reveal & CTA.
- **When to use:** Building trust and showcasing craftsmanship.
- **Common mistake:** Poor pacing; holding shots of repetitive tasks too long.

#### Day-in-the-Life / Narrative
- **Lengths:** 60s.
- **Beat Sheet:** 
  - *0-3s:* Hook (Morning routine, entering a space).
  - *3-15s:* Context (Establishing the environment).
  - *15-45s:* Journey (Interacting with the product/space).
  - *45-60s:* Resolution (Satisfaction, lifestyle payoff).
- **When to use:** Real estate tours, lifestyle branding.
- **Common mistake:** Lacking a clear narrative arc or emotional payoff.

### 3. Interior / Fit-out / Real-estate Specific
Strong studios tell a project story by showing the transformation (empty shell → finished), using light as a narrator (shadows moving across a room), emphasizing material close-ups (wood grain, marble veining), and incorporating human scale (a person interacting with the space).

### 4. Text and sound in the story
- **On-Screen Text Rules:** Keep it punchy (3-5 words per scene). Avoid relying on sound.
- **Sound-Off Viewing:** Design for silence. Visuals and text must communicate the full message, as many users scroll with sound off.
- **Voice-over vs Music-only:** VO with auto-captions increases retention by maintaining a human connection and pacing. Music-only works for highly visual montages but relies heavily on beat-synced cuts.
- **Arabic/English Considerations:** Arabic text (RTL) formatting ensures left-to-right alignment issues are avoided.

### 5. Brand moments
- **Early vs End:** For short-form ads, introduce the brand visually within the first 3 seconds (e.g., logo on a shirt, product in hand) without halting the story. End with a strong, static brand card for the final CTA.
- **CTA Patterns:** 
  - *Organic:* "Save this for later," "Comment X for the link." (Engagement-focused).
  - *Paid:* "Shop now," "Get a quote," direct and imperative. (Action-focused).


## PART 2 — Editing craft (montage)

### 1. Cut types and transitions
- **Hard Cut:** Instant shift between clips. (Says: continuity or stark contrast. When to use: standard narrative progression).
- **Match Cut:** Cutting on similar visual composition or action. (Says: thematic connection. When to use: transitioning between times or locations. Looks cheap if forced/misaligned).
- **J/L Cut:** Audio precedes visual (J) or visual precedes audio (L). (Says: seamless flow. When to use: interviews, B-roll overlays).
- **Jump Cut:** Cutting forward in time within the same shot. (Says: urgency, casual vlog style. Looks cheap if used to hide bad acting).
- **Whip/Motion-Match:** Fast pan cutting to another fast pan. (Says: dynamic energy. Looks cheap if overused in every transition).
- **Cross-Dissolve:** Fading between clips. (Says: passage of time. Looks cheap if used as a default transition for unrelated shots).

### 2. Rhythm and pacing
- **Shot Length Norms:** Short-form ads often average 1.5 to 2.5 seconds per shot to maintain the "change visuals every 3 seconds" rule. Project films allow longer, 3-5 second establishing shots.
- **Cutting to the Beat:** Mapping cuts to the BPM of the music (e.g., 120 BPM = 2 beats per second). At 24 fps, a cut every 12 frames creates a fast, rhythmic pace.
- **Tension & Release:** Build tension with rapid, tight cuts (ECU, fast motion) and release it with a longer, wider shot (the "breath" before the reveal).

### 3. Shot grammar
- **Sizes:** ECU (Extreme Close-Up - details/emotion) → CU (Close-Up - intimacy) → MS (Medium Shot - action/context) → WS (Wide Shot - environment) → EWS (Extreme Wide Shot - scale/location).
- **Angles:** Low angle (power/dominance), High angle (vulnerability), Eye-level (neutral/relatable).
- **Camera Moves:** Dolly (pushing in/out for emotional connection), Pan/Tilt (following action or revealing), Orbit (showcasing 3D space/hero object).
- **Sequence:** Standard (Wide → Medium → Close-up) establishes context. Alternative (Close-up → Medium → Wide) builds mystery and ends with a reveal.
- **Continuity:** Respect the 180° rule to avoid confusing the viewer's spatial awareness.

### 4. Murch's Rule of Six (Short-Form Application)
1. **Emotion (51%):** Does the cut make the viewer feel something? (Crucial for scroll-stopping).
2. **Story (23%):** Does it advance the 60-second narrative?
3. **Rhythm (10%):** Does it feel right? (Syncing with music/pacing).
4. **Eye Trace (7%):** Are you guiding the viewer's eye smoothly across the mobile screen?
5. **2D Plane (5%):** Respecting the 180-degree rule.
6. **3D Space (4%):** Spatial continuity. 
*In short-form, always prioritize Emotion and Rhythm; audiences will forgive spatial errors if the cut feels energetic and emotionally resonant.*

### 5. Finishing order
1. **Assembly:** Gather selects, rough timeline placement. (Review: Does the story exist?)
2. **Rough:** Pacing, structure, main cuts. (Review: Is the flow engaging?)
3. **Fine:** Tightening frames, adding J/L cuts. (Review: Is it perfectly timed?)
4. **Sound:** SFX, Foley, music mixing, VO. (Review: Is the audio immersive?)
5. **Colour:** Correction and grading. (Review: Does it match the brand/mood?)
6. **Titles:** Text overlays, checking margins. (Review: Are captions readable?)
7. **Export:** Formatting (9:16 / 16:9), compression.

### 6. Tools
- **CapCut:** Best for rapid social edits, templates, auto-captions. (Free / ~$10/mo Pro).
- **Premiere Pro:** Industry standard for assembly, multi-cam, team workflows. (~$23/mo).
- **After Effects:** Unmatched for complex motion graphics, compositing, VFX. (~$23/mo).
- **DaVinci Resolve:** Powerhouse for color grading and finishing. (Free / $295 Studio one-time).


## PART 3 — AI video production (pre-production → generation → post)

### 1. Pre-production for AI shots
**Storyboard Format:**
- **Shot #:** 1
- **Reference:** (Path to base image/sketch)
- **Start/End Frame:** First and last frame composition.
- **Camera Move:** e.g., "Slow push-in"
- **Duration:** 5s
- **Model:** Veo 3.1
- **Prompt:** "A sleek modern living room, slow dolly in, morning sunlight."
- **Negative Prompt:** "Morphing, extra limbs, low resolution, distortion."

### 2. Consistency across shots
- Maintain consistency by using identical base reference images across different prompts.
- Seed locking (reusing the same seed number) helps maintain style.
- What each model officially supports varies (e.g., character references before animating).

### 3. Prompting for camera motion in image-to-video
- **Kling:** Use structured formulas: Subject + Action + Scene + Camera (e.g., "low handheld tracking shot"). Use "locked-off" for static.
- **Veo:** Prefers natural cinematic prose (e.g., "from a fixed vantage point," "the camera dollies in slowly").
- **Runway:** Use built-in "Camera Control" sliders (pan, tilt, zoom) instead of text prompts for precise vectors. Use phased descriptions (e.g., "The camera arcs, then rises") to avoid drift.
- **General Rule:** Avoid stacking multiple camera moves. One move per clip prevents object morphing and flicker. Use modifiers like "slow" or "gentle."

### 5. Post for AI footage
- **Upscaling/Enhancing:** Topaz Video AI or Magnific (for adding detail and scaling to 4K).
- **Frame Interpolation:** Flowframes or Premiere's Optical Flow to smooth out choppy AI generation.
- **Deflicker:** DaVinci Resolve Studio’s Deflicker OFX or After Effects plugins to fix AI lighting inconsistencies.
- **Colour Matching:** Resolve’s Color Match tool to unify clips generated from different AI models.
- **Grain:** Adding a unified layer of film grain across all shots helps hide AI artifacts and binds the footage together.

### 6. Rights and disclosure
- **Meta (Instagram/Facebook):** Automatically applies "AI Info" labels for detected synthetic media (C2PA metadata). Manual self-disclosure is required for realistic social-issue/political ads.
- **TikTok:** Extremely strict. Requires visible labels on realistic AI-generated people/scenes. Uses C2PA detection. Failure to label results in suppression or removal.
- **YouTube:** Requires creator disclosure for photorealistic or "meaningfully altered" content. Applies labels automatically if creators fail to disclose significant AI use.
- **Commercial Use:** Varies by vendor tier (usually requires paid/Pro tiers for commercial rights; free tiers are often non-commercial).
