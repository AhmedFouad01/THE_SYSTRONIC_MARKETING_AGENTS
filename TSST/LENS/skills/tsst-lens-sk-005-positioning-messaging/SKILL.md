# TSST-LENS-SK-005 Positioning & Messaging

Status: `UNVALIDATED`

## Objective

Create positioning and a message hierarchy that make differentiated value clear to the best-fit audience and keep every material claim provable.

## Triggers

Use for positioning canvases, message houses, category choices, differentiator analysis, value propositions, claim review, or strategic narrative inputs.

## Reusable principles

- Start with what customers would do if the offer did not exist: direct products, manual work, status quo, or doing nothing.
- Identify capabilities only in relation to those alternatives.
- Translate differentiated capabilities into customer value using evidence.
- Select target segments that care most about the differentiated value.
- Choose a market category that makes the value understandable and credible.
- Attach proof and qualification to every important claim.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the product truth, customer evidence, decision, geography, and constraints.
2. List customer-observed competitive alternatives, including status quo.
3. Map capabilities that alternatives lack or deliver differently.
4. Translate each capability into a customer outcome and attach evidence.
5. Identify best-fit segments based on need and value, not aspiration alone.
6. Evaluate category contexts for clarity, expectations, and credibility.
7. Build a positioning canvas and message house: core promise, pillars, proof, objections, and qualified claims.
8. Test the message against evidence, alternatives, and likely misunderstanding.

## Output contract

- competitive alternatives;
- differentiated capabilities;
- customer value and evidence;
- best-fit audience and exclusions;
- category choice and tradeoffs;
- positioning statement/canvas;
- message hierarchy with proof and claim qualifications;
- open hypotheses and tests.

## Guardrails

- Every material claim must be provable or explicitly hypothetical.
- Do not start with a slogan and reverse-engineer evidence.
- Do not name a competitor the customer would not actually consider.
- Do not confuse a feature with differentiated value.
- Do not hide category tradeoffs or unsupported superlatives.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Alternatives reflect customer behavior.
- [ ] Differentiation is relative and evidence-backed.
- [ ] Value connects capability to a meaningful outcome.
- [ ] Audience selection is justified.
- [ ] Category makes the value easier to understand.
- [ ] Message pillars include proof and qualification.

## Practical evaluation

Build a positioning canvas and message house from an evaluator-supplied product evidence pack. The fixture includes internal feature claims, customer quotes, competitor alternatives, and one unprovable superlative. The candidate must remove or qualify unsupported claims.

## Common failure modes

- vague “for everyone” audience;
- competitor list unrelated to customer choice;
- differentiator that competitors also claim;
- value asserted without evidence;
- category selected for trend appeal;
- slogan mistaken for positioning.
