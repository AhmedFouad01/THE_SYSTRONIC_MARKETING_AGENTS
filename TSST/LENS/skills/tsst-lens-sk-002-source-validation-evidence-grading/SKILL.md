# TSST-LENS-SK-002 Source Validation & Evidence Grading

Status: `UNVALIDATED`

## Objective

Decide whether a source is suitable for a specific claim and communicate confidence without turning source reputation into a substitute for evidence quality.

## Triggers

Use when validating sources, reviewing research claims, resolving conflicting evidence, checking freshness, or building an evidence pack for strategy or presentations.

## Reusable principles

- Grade the source for the claim being made; no source has one universal grade.
- Prefer primary evidence and official current platform documentation where relevant.
- Assess authority, directness, methodology, freshness, scope fit, bias/conflict, and corroboration separately.
- Distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence.
- Preserve conflicting credible sources and explain the likely cause of disagreement.
- Confidence is a reasoned judgment tied to evidence, not a decorative label.

## Workflow

1. Break the deliverable into material claims.
2. Record each source's author/publisher, date, retrieval date, source type, method, population, geography, and incentives.
3. Assess how directly the source supports each claim.
4. Check freshness against the volatility of the subject.
5. Review method transparency, sample, definitions, exclusions, and limitations.
6. Seek independent corroboration for important claims.
7. Grade each claim-source relationship and state the rationale and residual uncertainty.
8. Downgrade, narrow, relabel, or remove claims that the evidence cannot support.

## Output contract

- claim-evidence ledger;
- source metadata and classification;
- criterion-by-criterion grade and rationale;
- conflict/corroboration notes;
- claim disposition: supported, qualified, assumption, hypothesis, disputed, or unsupported;
- missing-evidence plan.

## Guardrails

- Every important claim needs locatable evidence or an explicit non-fact label.
- Do not assign confidence from domain name, citation count, or brand recognition alone.
- Do not use a recent article to mask an old underlying dataset.
- Do not average contradictory sources without reconciling definitions and scope.
- The archived NASA page may guide structure but receives a freshness limitation for current practice.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Claims are atomic enough to evaluate.
- [ ] Source metadata and retrieval dates are recorded.
- [ ] Directness, method, freshness, scope, and bias are assessed.
- [ ] Important claims have corroboration or a documented reason they do not.
- [ ] Conflicts are preserved and analyzed.
- [ ] Unsupported claims are removed or relabeled.

## Practical evaluation

Rank ten evaluator-supplied sources for five specific claims. The set must include an official but outdated page, a recent secondary summary of old data, a transparent primary study, an opaque vendor study, conflicting definitions, and duplicated reporting of one original source.

## Common failure modes

- one global score per source;
- recency used as the only quality criterion;
- multiple articles mistaken for independent corroboration;
- citation present but irrelevant to the claim;
- limitations omitted from the final claim;
- unsupported certainty language.
