# TSST-LENS-SK-001 Research Planning

Status: `UNVALIDATED`

## Objective

Design research that starts with the decision to support and produces evidence capable of answering explicit questions within time, access, and quality constraints.

## Triggers

Use when framing a market, customer, competitor, positioning, or marketing research brief; selecting methods; defining evidence needs; or rescuing an unbounded request to “research the market.”

## Reusable principles

- Begin with the decision, not with search activity.
- Separate the business question, research questions, hypotheses, and deliverables.
- Choose methods because they fit the question and evidence needed.
- Define population, geography, period, units, and exclusions before collection.
- Record source/data strengths, limitations, and uncertainty.
- Plan how findings will be analyzed and communicated before gathering data.

## Workflow

1. State the decision, decision owner, deadline, and action alternatives.
2. Translate the decision into a small set of answerable research questions.
3. Record current facts, assumptions, hypotheses, and unknowns separately.
4. Define scope: market, audience, geography, time, product, competitors, and exclusions.
5. Build an evidence plan mapping each question to needed evidence, preferred source type, method, and fallback.
6. Define sampling or selection logic, analysis method, quality checks, and limitations.
7. Specify deliverables, claim-evidence traceability, and decision criteria.
8. Run a feasibility review and remove work that cannot affect the decision.

## Output contract

- decision statement and owner;
- research questions and hypotheses;
- scope and definitions;
- evidence/method matrix;
- sample or source-selection logic;
- analysis and QA plan;
- deliverables, timeline, dependencies, limitations, and stop conditions.

## Guardrails

- Do not begin with indiscriminate information collection.
- Do not call a research question a fact or a hypothesis a finding.
- Do not promise representativeness without a defensible sample.
- Do not hide missing access, data, or time constraints.
- Treat the NASA guide as an archived structure reference, not current marketing-research authority.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] The supported decision is explicit.
- [ ] Questions are answerable and non-duplicative.
- [ ] Scope, population, period, and exclusions are defined.
- [ ] Each question maps to evidence and a method.
- [ ] Quality checks and limitations are planned.
- [ ] Deliverables support the decision rather than generic reporting.

## Practical evaluation

Design a research brief for entry into an evaluator-specified new market. The fixture includes a fixed deadline, limited first-party data, two plausible methods, and one unanswerable request. The candidate must prioritize, justify methods, and expose the unanswerable item.

## Common failure modes

- broad topic list instead of research questions;
- methods chosen because they are available;
- no population or time boundary;
- deliverables disconnected from the decision;
- unsupported promise of statistical confidence;
- collection plan with no analysis plan.
