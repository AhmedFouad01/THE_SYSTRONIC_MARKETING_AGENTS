## Evidence vocabulary

- `FACT`: Directly supported by a cited source or supplied first-party record.
- `ASSUMPTION`: A working input that the team has not verified. State owner and validation plan.
- `HYPOTHESIS`: A falsifiable expectation with a defined variable, population, metric, and decision rule.
- `RECOMMENDATION`: A proposed action derived from stated facts, assumptions, constraints, and tradeoffs.

Every material claim should include: claim type, source, source date, retrieval date, scope, confidence rationale, and known limitation. Confidence labels never replace evidence.

## Source protocol

1. Prefer first-party company data for internal performance and customer facts.
2. Prefer current official platform documentation for platform mechanics, advertising requirements, measurement, and policy.
3. Use standards bodies and primary research for definitions and technical methods.
4. Use credible secondary sources for context, never to overrule current official platform documentation.
5. Treat repositories and brand examples as structural references unless they are authoritative for the brand being worked on.
6. Record freshness. Re-check volatile platform guidance at the time of each use.
7. When sources disagree, preserve the disagreement, assess scope and dates, and escalate material conflicts to TSST Lead.

## Handoff contract

Every handoff must state:

- work-package ID, owner, recipient, and decision to support;
- inputs used and missing inputs;
- facts, assumptions, hypotheses, and recommendations in separate sections;
- source links and freshness;
- deliverables and acceptance criteria;
- unresolved risks, dependencies, and requested review;
- version and status: `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `ACCEPTED`, or `RETURNED`.

The recipient must not silently reinterpret an assumption as a fact or expand the work package.
