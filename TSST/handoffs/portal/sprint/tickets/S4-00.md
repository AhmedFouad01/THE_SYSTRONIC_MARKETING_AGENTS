## Plan wording
One maker per item (also before anything is uploaded); per client: a reviewer, then the approver (the client's
approver, or a team member); decisions recorded by step and by person; the maker cannot decide on their own item;
"Planned" until the maker sends.

## What this ticket delivers (said plainly)
- Every item can have one named maker. An item the maker has not sent is **Planned**: it is in nobody's review list.
- Per client, the owner sets a **reviewer** (a team member) and may name the client's **approver** (a client person).
  The review order is: maker sends, reviewer decides, approver decides.
- A decision row says which step it was (review or approval) and who made it (a person id, not only a name).
- The maker cannot decide on their own item. The server refuses it.
- One function, `turnOf` / `turnsOf`, says for every item "which step, with whom, since when". Home, Tasks, the Job
  page and the Review queues (S4-01, 03, 04) read it and nothing else.
- **A client with no reviewer set behaves exactly as today** until the owner sets one. The whole new behaviour is
  switched on per client by that one field.
- Not here: the screens (S4-01, 03, 04), Tasks (S4-08), the Schedule gate and its exception (S4-03 enforces them; this
  ticket only provides the facts and reserves the event name), the in-house workspace (postponed, but possible later
  with no second migration of these tables), notifications (nothing here sends anyone anything).

(`job-model.ts:157-164`). A team decision that is not an approval takes the version back from the client
(`:245`). There is no step "the maker sends for review": that is the new thing.

## 2. The model

### 2.1 Words
- **Maker**: the one team member who owns making the item. Nullable (an item nobody owns yet).
- **Reviewer**: per client, one team member who decides the first step.
- **Approver**: per client, who decides the second step. Core: the client's approver (a client person, or any approver
  of the client). In-house (later): a team member.
- **Step**: `review` (reviewer) or `approval` (approver). A decision row's step is fixed by its track:
  `team` track = step `review`; `client` track = step `approval`. **`track` keeps meaning "the lane" and is not
  renamed** (a rename would touch every reader of the old and new tables). In the in-house variant the approval step is
  still the `client` lane, made by a team user: the person columns say who.
- **Ordered client**: a client whose `reviewer_user_id` is not null. Everything new is enforced only for ordered clients.

Why a generated `step`: the append-only trigger forbids an `UPDATE` backfill of old rows (1.3); a generated column gives
every old and new row its step with no write to history, and the old code (which inserts without the column) keeps
working. The table is small (a handful of clients); the rewrite it needs is measured in the rehearsal (row count and
lock time recorded in the pull request).

Old code on the new schema (deploy order and `--rollback`): it inserts decisions and events without the new columns
(all nullable, constant default, or generated), reads `i.*` and ignores new fields, and never inserts an event action
outside `sent` / `withdrawn`. Nothing it needs is removed.

### 2.3 The item's step machine
`ordered` = the client's `reviewer_user_id` is set. For the **current version** `v` of a live item (not deleted, job
not archived, not delivered), with `T` = the team decision on `v`, `C` = the client decision on `v`:
`submitted(v)` = `ordered ? submitted_version = v : true` (an unordered client has no Planned state: as today).

Evaluated top to bottom, the first line that matches is the step:

| # | Condition | Step | Status word (unchanged words, SPEC section 2) | With whom (who must act) | Who may act, and the action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `live_version = v` (approver approved the team-approved version) | `ready` for a post whose life is `in_progress`; `done` otherwise (scheduled, posted, delivered, ad lives, and every non-post item) | Approved / Scheduled / Posted / Delivered | `ready`: the maker (to schedule). `done`: nobody | `ready`: schedule (S4-03 gates it, see 2.8) |
| 2 | not `submitted(v)` | `planned` | Planned | the maker | the maker or an owner: **submit** (action `submit`) |
| 3 | `T` is null or `pending` | `review` | Pending review | the effective reviewer (2.6) | an eligible reviewer: approve / request changes (team decision). The maker or an owner: **recall** (back to Planned) while no decision exists |
| 4 | `T` is `changes_requested` | `changes` (source `review`) | Changes requested | the maker | the maker (or an owner): make a new version, then submit it |
| 5 | `T` is `approved` and the item is not sent (`hold_until_all` holds it back, or it is withdrawn) | `held` | Pending review (held) | nobody named; the items that block it are in their own steps | an owner may release early (existing `release-early`, unchanged) |
| 6 | sent, and `C` is null or `pending` | `approval` | Waiting on client (approver is a client) / Waiting on approval (approver is a team member, in-house later) | the approver (2.6) | an approver: approve / request changes (client route) |
| 7 | sent, and `C` is `changes_requested` | `changes` (source `approval`) | Changes requested | the maker | as row 4 |

Notes:
- **Planned is a state of the current version.** A new version (file bytes changed, a Dash edit) has no submission, so
  in an ordered client it is Planned again, whatever the previous version did. The Drive sync makes versions by itself;
  it must never put work in front of a reviewer without the maker's act. This is deliberate (question 4).
- `withdrawn` (a team member took the item back from the client) shows as step 5 `held` with the reason `withdrawn`,
  as today: any team decision ends it.
- One version has exactly one step. That is what lets health and Home not count the same item twice (section 5).
- Existing meaning of `sent_version`, `live_version`, `withdrawn`, `hold_until_all` is **unchanged**. The only new
  state is `submitted_version`. A team approval still releases the item to the client in the same transaction
  (`releaseItems`): "reviewer approves" and "the item reaches the approver" are one act, as today.

Transitions (all inside `decideItem` / the three new writers, under the job's lock):

| Action | From step | To step | Writes |
|---|---|---|---|
| submit | planned | review | `items.submitted_version = v`; event `submitted` (`by`, `by_user_id`) |
| recall | review (no team decision on `v`) | planned | `submitted_version = NULL`; event `recalled` |
| review: approve | review | approval (or `held`) | decision row (track team, step review); then `releaseItems` as today |
| review: request changes | review | changes | decision row (note required, as today); `sent_version = NULL` as today |
| approval: approve | approval | ready / done | decision row (track client); `live_version = v` as today |
| approval: request changes | approval | changes | decision row (note required) |
| new version | any | planned | `saveItem` as today; `submitted_version` is left as it is and no longer equals `current_version` |

### 2.4 Where "Planned" fits
Planned is **not a stored status word**; it is step 2 above, computed. The status words of SPEC section 2 do not change;
"Planned" / "متخطط" is the one new word. In an unordered client no item is ever Planned (as today, where every item is
"pending"). `submitted_version` is also recorded for unordered clients when a team member presses send (harmless, and
exact if the owner later switches the client on).

### 2.5 How a decision row names its step and its person
| Column | Value on a new row | On an old row |
|---|---|---|
| `step` (generated) | `review` for track team, `approval` for track client | the same, computed |
| `by` (text, as today) | the name shown (user name / client label) | as it was |
| `by_user_id` | the team user who decided; null for a client | null (**not backfilled**: a name is not an identity, and the table is append-only) |
| `by_person_id` | the client person (role approver) who decided; null for a team user and for an old link session | null |
| `self_decided` | true only when the one-person-team exception (2.7) was used | false |

An old link session has neither id (only its label in `by`): it stays "by name". The same two ids go on `item_events`
(`by_user_id`) for submit / recall.

### 2.6 How the next person is computed (the "with whom" for every state)
Let `active(u)` = the user exists, `disabled = false`, `deleted_at IS NULL`.
- **Effective reviewer** of a client:
  1. unordered client: `with` = the earliest-created active owner (so a person is named); **eligible** = every active
     team member (as today).
  2. ordered, reviewer active and reviewer is not the item's maker: `with` = eligible = the reviewer.
  3. ordered, reviewer inactive **or** reviewer is the item's maker: `base` = every active owner; `with` = the earliest
     of them that is not the maker; eligible = `base` minus the maker.
  4. if `eligible` is empty and the team has exactly one active member: eligible = that member (the maker), the
     decision is marked `self_decided` (2.7).
  5. if `eligible` is empty otherwise (the owner is both maker and the only candidate): `with` = null and
     `blocked = 'no_eligible_reviewer'`. Nobody can decide until the owner names another reviewer for the client or
     hands the item to another maker. The read model reports it so Home can tell the owner.
- **Effective maker** (for steps planned, changes, ready): the maker if active; if the maker is null: `with` = null,
  `unassigned = true` (a Planned item with no maker is in nobody's week; the owner sees a count); if the maker is
  inactive: `with` = the earliest active owner, `maker_inactive = true`; eligible = all active owners.
- **Approver** (step approval): core: `with` = the named client person (`approver_person_id`, active, role approver) if
  set, else `{kind:'client', name: <client display name>}`. **Eligible = every active client person with role approver
  and every old link session of the client, as today**, named or not (question 1). In-house later (no code now):
  `with` = `approver_user_id`, eligible = that user.
- `since` (for the age): planned = version `created_at`; review = the `submitted` event time (ordered) or the version's
  `created_at` (unordered, as the queue sorts today); changes = the decision's `at`; approval = the version's `sent_at`
  (as `clientReviewFacts` uses); ready = the client decision's `at`.

### 2.7 The rule "the maker cannot decide on their own item"
Enforced in `decideItem`, under the job lock, only for an **ordered** client, for the `team` track (the client track has
no maker: a client person is never a team user). The maker is `items.maker_user_id`; the actor is the signed-in team user
(`c.user.id`), compared by id, never by name.

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| The actor is the maker and other people are eligible | refused: 403 `maker_cannot_decide` |
| The actor is the maker and is the owner, and the owner is also the client's reviewer, other members exist | refused 403 `maker_cannot_decide`; the item is `blocked` (2.6.5); fix = name another reviewer or hand the item over |
| The reviewer is also the maker | the item's reviewer falls to the owners (2.6.3); if the maker is the only owner, the same as the line above |
| One-person team (exactly one active team member, the maker) | allowed, row marked `self_decided = true`; Activity can show "self-reviewed" |
| The maker is deactivated | cannot decide (cannot sign in); the item is with an owner (`maker_inactive`); an owner reassigns |
| The item has no maker (null) | no restriction (nobody to exclude); `unassigned` is shown |
| The actor is not eligible (not the reviewer, not an owner standing in) | refused: 403 `not_your_turn` |
| Unordered client | none of this applies (as today) |

An owner who is not the reviewer cannot decide a step that is not theirs (they may change the reviewer first). The one
way around the order is the owner changing the client's reviewer or the item's maker. The reviewer change is recorded
(`client_settings.updated_by` / `updated_at`); the maker change is not (3.4).

### 2.8 Facts for scheduling (used by S4-03, not enforced here)
`scheduleGate(turn)` returns `{ allowed, reason }`: allowed when the client is unordered (as today), or the step is
`ready` (approved by the approver). S4-03 puts it in front of the schedule route and writes the one exception as an
`item_events` row `schedule_exception` with a required note (the action name is already allowed by migration 036).

### 3.3 The moment the owner sets the first reviewer (the switch-on)
In the transaction of `PUT /client/settings` that changes `reviewer_user_id` from NULL to a user, under every job
lock of the client (locked in sorted job order), for each live item of the client whose current version is not already
submitted: set `submitted_version = current_version` and write a `submitted` event (by the owner, note
`review order switched on`) **when the item has any decision on the current version, or is sent, or has a live
version, or has at least one file, or its life is not `in_progress`.** The rest (nothing uploaded, nothing decided) stay
Planned. This is the rule that keeps today's queue intact while taking the empty planned posts out of it (P1).
The response says how many items were marked and how many stay Planned. It is one function, tested on its own, used only
here. Setting the reviewer from one user to another changes nothing in the data. Setting it to NULL turns the
behaviour off again (marks stay, ignored).

Items already with the client at the switch: step `approval`, unchanged. Items approved before the migration: step
`ready` or `done`, unchanged. Items with the client's `changes_requested`: step `changes`, with the maker if known,
else `unassigned`.

### 3.4 Makers and creation after the migration
New items get a maker where a person made them. The item of a post made in the Dash is made later by the sync, so
`syncPostItem` sets the maker from `posts.created_by` by the same unique-active-name rule, else the job's
`default_maker_user_id`, else NULL. An item made by a route (`work.ts:326`) takes the signed-in user; the plan import takes
the importing user. **A change of maker is not recorded as history** in this ticket: `item_events` is about
versions and is not widened for it, and a separate list costs more than it is worth for a handful of items. The row
holds the current maker only. Said here so the review can ask for it: if a history is wanted, it is one more
event name in the same CHECK (cheap to add now, in PR a, if the reviewer asks).

## 5. Read model: who must act
One module `src/service/dash/turn.ts`. A pure core (testable without a database) and one loader.

### 5.1 Inputs
```
turnsOf(db, world, { brand?, job?, itemIds?, today }) -> ItemTurn[]
turnOf(item, ctx) -> ItemTurn           // pure: ctx = the client's order, the users, the people, the job facts
```
`ctx` is built by one loader query set (for the brands in scope): `client_settings` review columns, the active team users
(id, name, role, active), the active client approver people, and per job `archived` / `deleted` / `delivered` / project
hidden (the same facts `clientReviewFacts` reads, `health.ts:91-101`). Items come from `world.items`
(`modelItems`, which already returns every column, so the new item columns arrive without a new query).

### 5.2 Output
```
ItemTurn {
  item_id, brand, job, key, title, type, version, publish_on: string|null,
  ordered: boolean,
  step: 'planned'|'review'|'approval'|'changes'|'ready'|'held'|'done',
  changes_source: 'review'|'approval'|null,
  status: 'planned'|'pending_review'|'waiting_on_client'|'waiting_on_approval'|'changes_requested'|'approved'|'scheduled'|'posted'|'delivered'|...,   // the SPEC words
  with: { kind: 'user'|'client_person'|'client', id: string|null, name: string, inactive?: true } | null,
  eligible: { users: string[], client: 'approvers'|'person'|null },
  since: ISO|null, days: number,       // whole Cairo days, daysSince(health.ts:56)
  maker: { id, name, active } | null,
  blocked: 'no_eligible_reviewer' | null,
  unassigned: boolean,                 // a step that needs a maker and has none
  maker_inactive: boolean,
  self_review_allowed: boolean         // the one-person-team case
}
```
`done` rows are returned only when asked (`includeDone`), so Home's default call is small. Items of archived or deleted
jobs, delivered jobs and hidden projects: step `done` or left out exactly as `clientReviewFacts` leaves them out.

Helpers, also pure: `needsAction(turns, userId)` (rows whose `eligible.users` contains the user **and** `with` is that
user: P1's "a named person must act now"), `waitingOnOthers(turns, userId)` (the user is the maker or the one who sent it
to review, and `with` is someone else: the quiet "3 of your items are with Ahmed Samir" line and the Review tab),
`scheduleGate(turn)` (2.8).

### 5.3 How health and "waiting" relate, without double counting
- A version has one step, so one item is counted once everywhere.
- "Waiting on the client" (health, `waiting`) is exactly the turns with step `approval` whose `with.kind` is `client`
  or `client_person`, in a job the client can decide in. Planned, review, changes and held items are never in it
  (already so today: they are not `itemVisible`). A team-member approver (in-house later) is `waiting_on_approval`
  and is **not** counted as waiting on the client.
- "Owed" (health's `changes_owed`) = turns with step `changes`, `changes_source = 'approval'`.
- S4-00 leaves `clientReviewFacts` as it is and adds a **parity test** (for an unordered and an ordered client, the number
  of `approval` turns equals the number of facts with `waiting` not null, and the owed ones agree). S4-01 moves the
  health word onto `turnsOf` in its own pull request, so there is one definition in the end and it is never in two
  places without a test in the meantime.
- Home's "Needs action" rows are `needsAction`; the Review tab "Needs your review" is `needsAction` with step `review`.
  A row appears on Home only when `with` is a named person; `unassigned` and `blocked` items appear in a separate small
  count for the owner (S4-01 draws it; this ticket returns the facts).

## 7. Options rejected
| Option | Why not |
|---|---|
| **A list of approvers per item** (each item carries its own ordered list of people) | The brief and Decision 18 say the order is per client. A per-item list is a table of rows for every item of every client, hard to see and to change, and it makes "who is next" depend on rows nobody maintains. Per-client order is two columns and one place to look. The one thing a per-item list gives (a different reviewer for one item) is covered by changing the item's maker or, later, an override column if it is asked for. |
| **A generic workflow engine** (states, transitions and guards as data) | There are two fixed steps and one extra state. An engine is a second thing to test and to learn, and it would put the rules in data where S2-03's one writer cannot see them. The step machine here is one short function with a table-driven test. The in-house variant needs the same two steps with another kind of approver, which the columns already allow. |
| **Reusing roles alone** (a "reviewer" role next to owner and team) | A role is global to the workspace; the order is per client. The same person reviews one client and only makes work for another. It would also add a role value to `portal."user".role`, which `guard.ts` and `auth.ts` treat as two values only. |
| **A new `step` column that replaces `track`** | Touches every reader of `item_decisions`, `current_item_decisions`, `decision_history`, the client payloads and the tests of S2-03. A generated `step` column names the step with no change to readers. |
| **Backfilling `by_user_id` on old decisions** | The table is append-only, and a name in a text column is not an identity (two users may share a name; a name changes). A wrong id in an audit trail is worse than none. |
| **Making "Planned" a stored status** | Two places would have to agree (a status and `submitted_version`); a new version would need a reset write. A computed state cannot drift. |
| **Enforce the new rules for every client at once** | A client with items in flight and a maker guessed from a log would be blocked on the day of the deploy. Per-client switch-on is the only way to keep "nothing that works today may stop". |
| **The sync auto-submits a new version of an already submitted item** | The sync changes versions on its own; a half-saved file would go to the reviewer. Sending is the maker's act. |
| **A trigger to refuse a maker's decision in SQL** | S2-03 chose one writer and removed the dependence on triggers; the rule needs the client's order and the user list, so it belongs in `decideItem`. |
