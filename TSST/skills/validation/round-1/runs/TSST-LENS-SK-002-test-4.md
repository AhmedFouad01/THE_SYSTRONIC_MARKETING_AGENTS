# Test Run: TSST-LENS-SK-002 · Test 4

## Prompt
Grade sources and evaluate the evidence for the following claims regarding luxury residential interior fit-out budgets in Greater Cairo (New Cairo & Sheikh Zayed) for a boutique interior architecture studio:
1. "Homeowners receiving units in premium gated compounds (e.g., Palm Hills, SODIC, Emaar Misr) spend an average of 4,500,000 EGP on interior design, finishing, and custom joinery."
2. "Over 80% of compound buyers hire an independent interior designer before developer handover to customize MEP and layout partitions."
3. "Turnkey delivery delays exceed 6 months for more than 70% of residential finishing contracts in Cairo."

Sources provided:
- Source X: Real Estate Market Intelligence Report by JLL MENA (Dated: Q4 2024; retrieved 2026-10-09; professional real estate consultancy market overview).
- Source Y: Egyptian Interior Designers Syndicate & Chamber of Woodworking Industries (CWI) Industry Whitepaper (Dated: February 2025; retrieved 2026-10-08; member survey covering 85 turnkey design contractors).
- Source Z: Luxury Villa Finishing Broker Instagram Guide & Article (Dated: August 2026; retrieved 2026-10-10; promotional content marketing article by a local turnkey contractor).

### 4. Conflict & Corroboration Notes
- **Budget Scope & Unit Size Fallacy (Claim 4.1):** Source Z states 4.5M EGP as a universal average, but Source Y clarifies that finishing costs range from 14,000 to 22,000 EGP per square meter for ultra-luxury finishes. While a 250 sqm villa easily requires 4M–5M EGP, a 160 sqm apartment in Palm Hills averages 2.2M–2.8M EGP. Stating a flat 4.5M EGP without defining unit area or specification level is misleading.
- **Pre-Handover Engagement Rate (Claim 4.2):** The 80% claim is refuted by Source Y. Most developers (SODIC, Emaar) enforce strict freeze periods on exterior/MEP alterations prior to official unit delivery, forcing the majority (64%) of homeowners to commence design and demolition work only after receiving the keys.
