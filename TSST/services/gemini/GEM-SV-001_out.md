# PART 1 — The identity process (new vs rebrand)

Professional branding agencies follow a structured process to ensure the visual identity solves a business problem rather than just looking good. 

**Stages from Brief to Handover:**
1. **Discovery & Research:** Agencies conduct stakeholder interviews, market analysis, and competitor benchmarking. For a rebrand, an audit is performed first to assess existing brand equity, audience perception, and where the current identity fails. 
2. **Strategy & Positioning:** Defining the brand's core purpose, values, personality, and optimal market positioning. 
3. **Creative Development:** This phase translates strategy into visual assets. It starts with mood boards to align on the "feel," followed by the creation of the logo, color palette, typography, and visual system. Agencies typically present 2–3 distinct design directions.
4. **Refinement & Client Approval:** The client selects a direction, which undergoes refinement. Usually, agencies build in 2–3 rounds of revisions to avoid scope creep. 
5. **Implementation & Guidelines:** The finalized identity is rolled out across various touchpoints (digital, social, print). A comprehensive brand guideline book is created for handover.

**Timelines:** A full agency branding process typically takes 6 to 12 weeks, depending on the scale and client feedback speed.

# PART 2 — Brand guidelines book

A professional brand guideline ensures consistency for future use. The standard order of sections is usually:
1. **Purpose & Strategy:** Mission, vision, and core values.
2. **Personality & Voice:** Tone of voice and messaging pillars.
3. **Logo:** Primary logo, secondary variations, clear space, minimum size, and explicit misuse examples.
4. **Colour:** Primary and secondary palettes with exact values for HEX, RGB, CMYK, and Pantone.
5. **Typography:** Primary and secondary typefaces, font hierarchy, and weights.
6. **Imagery/Art Direction:** Photography style, illustrations, and graphic treatments.
7. **Graphic Devices:** Patterns, shapes, icons, and UI elements.
8. **Layouts and Applications:** Examples for social media, stationery, packaging, etc.
9. **Accessibility:** Contrast ratios and legible font rules.

**Professional Kit Contents & Handover:**
A professional social kit is delivered as an organized cloud folder containing:
*   **Ready-to-use Files:** High-res PNG/JPGs for profile pictures, covers, and highlight icons appropriately named (e.g., `Brand_IG_Avatar_2026.png`).
*   **Templates:** Editable PSD, AI, or Figma files for story, reel, and post templates. 
*   **Source Construction:** Organized layers, embedded smart objects for easy photo swapping, and locked brand elements to prevent client errors.
*   **Handover Method:** Agencies provide a brief "How-to" PDF or record a Loom video explaining how to utilize the templates without violating brand guidelines. A text file containing bio conventions and pinned post strategies is often included.

# PART 4 — Applying the identity

*   **Template Consistency:** A template system stays consistent without being repetitive by utilizing a fixed structural grid, restricted typography hierarchy, and a strict color palette. Variety is introduced by changing the imagery, content, and alternating background colors within the palette.
*   **Logo Use on Social:** Use the logo sparingly on feed posts. Since the brand's profile picture is always visible next to the post, slapping a large logo on every asset is redundant. 
*   **Photo Treatment:** Implement strict rules for photography—such as using consistent color overlays, specific contrast levels, or applying a subtle grain effect to unify different photos (e.g., Airbnb's consistent photographic warmth).
*   **Arabic/RTL Considerations:** Text alignment in templates must flip to the right. More importantly, platform UI elements on Stories/Reels (like heart/comment buttons on the right) mean the safe zones must be strictly mirrored. 
*   **Examples:** TikTok's explicit division of RTL safe zones (https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/in-feed-ads-safe-zones) and DesignStudio's varied but unified photo system for Airbnb (https://design.studio/work/airbnb).

# PART 5 — Quality checklist + common mistakes

**20-Item QA Checklist:**
1. Does the primary logo scale down legibly for social avatars?
2. Is the logo perfectly centered for circle-cropping?
3. Do RGB and HEX color values exactly match the defined brand palette?
4. Are HEX codes verified for web accessibility contrast (WCAG)?
5. Are web fonts and fallback fonts clearly defined?
6. Are English and Arabic typography styles documented?
7. Are RTL text alignments set correctly in templates?
8. Are Facebook cover photos checked against both mobile and desktop crop zones?
9. Do LinkedIn and X headers avoid the bottom-left profile picture overlap area?
10. Are Reel/Story templates clear of the platform UI (top and bottom)?
11. Are Instagram highlight covers exported as ready-to-upload files?
12. Are working template files (Figma/PSD) intuitively organized?
13. Are template text layers fully editable for the client?
14. Are static brand elements locked to prevent accidental client edits?
15. Is the photography style explicitly defined (do's and don'ts)?
16. Are visual examples provided for logo and color "misuse"?
17. Is the brand voice and tone section complete?
18. Are final exports compressed under platform file size limits?
19. Did the creative director do a final visual QA on a mobile device?
20. Is the handover folder structure intuitive and clearly labeled?

**10 Common Mistakes & Fixes:**
1. **Mistake:** Ignoring circle crops for avatars. **Fix:** Use circular mask guides in the export templates.
2. **Mistake:** Designing headers only for desktop. **Fix:** Always preview on mobile or use safe zone templates that account for both.
3. **Mistake:** Overusing logos on feed posts. **Fix:** Rely on brand colors and typography for recognition instead.
4. **Mistake:** Inaccessible color combinations. **Fix:** Run a WCAG contrast checker before finalizing the palette.
5. **Mistake:** RTL overlap with platform UI. **Fix:** Flip safe zone guides horizontally for Arabic content.
6. **Mistake:** Unorganized template layers. **Fix:** Group, name, and color-code layers logically.
7. **Mistake:** Inconsistent RGB vs CMYK. **Fix:** Manually define color values; don't rely on auto-conversion.
8. **Mistake:** Missing font licenses. **Fix:** Provide links for the client to purchase or download open-source fonts.
9. **Mistake:** Handing over files without instruction. **Fix:** Record a short screen-share video showing how to use the kit.
10. **Mistake:** Relying on outdated platform specs. **Fix:** Always verify dimensions against official platform help pages.

# PART 6 — Playbook rule candidates

1. Always present visual identities as a scalable system, not just a logo. (Source: Pentagram case studies)
2. Strategy must precede design; define positioning before creating mood boards. (Source: Koto & Collins process)
3. Rebrand audits must document existing brand equity before proposing any visual changes. (Source: Ragged Edge)
4. Brand guidelines must define "misuse" as strictly as proper use. (Source: Uber Brand Guidelines)
5. Accessibility (WCAG contrast) must be validated for all core color pairings. (Source: Atlassian Design)
6. Social avatars must be tested against a circular crop before client approval. (Source: Instagram Help)
7. Cover photos must respect both mobile and desktop safe zones simultaneously. (Source: Facebook Help)
8. Reel/Story templates must leave the bottom 35% and top 14% empty for platform UI. (Source: Instagram Help)
9. RTL template designs must explicitly mirror UI safe zones to avoid text overlap. (Source: TikTok Safe Zones)
10. LinkedIn and X header designs must leave the bottom-left quadrant empty for avatar overlap. (Source: LinkedIn Help)
11. Final templates must have named layers and locked brand assets to protect the identity. (Source: Agency Handover standards)
12. Social post designs should avoid redundant logo placement since the avatar is always present. (Source: DesignStudio)
13. Always deliver a "how-to" asset (video or document) alongside the template files. (Source: Best practices)
